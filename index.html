What the assessment decides

Five things get settled by the assessment and nowhere else. If you are asked why it is worth the effort, this is the answer.

Which standards you report

Each of the ten topical standards opens only if a matter under it is material. Everything else stays closed, and you are entitled to keep it closed.

How deep each disclosure goes

The lens that made a matter material, its subtopic and its severity set how much you say about it. Under ESRS 2026 you report only the material subtopic, not the whole standard.

What data you collect, and from whom

Material matters in the value chain mean supplier or customer data. Not material means no request goes out. The assessment is your data budget.

What the assurer tests

Limited assurance covers the process, not only the numbers. The assurer reads your method note, thresholds and evidence before any metric.

What management has to answer for

Every material matter must be tied to strategy and have a policy, action and target, or a plain statement that there is none yet.

Your GRI and ISSB lists too

Score both lenses once to the ESRS standard and the GRI topic list and the ISSB risk list fall out as filters of the same register.

What you should have on file when you finish

Seven files. If you have these, you have done a double materiality assessment, whatever route you took. The workbook on this page produces the fourth and drafts the sixth.

ESRS 1, chapter 3, and EFRAG IG 1

Run the assessment

Four steps. For each: what you do, who needs to be in the room, the file you keep, the question the assurer will ask about it, and the usual way it goes wrong.

Bottom-up route

List individual impacts, risks and opportunities across every topic, score each, then roll up to topics. Thorough, slow, and what most first-wave reporters did under ESRS 2023. Still valid, and still the better route for topics where you expect a fight over the conclusion.

Top-down route, endorsed in ESRS 2026

Start from the topic list and your business model. Rule out topics that plainly don't apply, with a reason. Conclude at topic level where the answer is obvious either way. Only go down to individual matters where it is a judgement call. You may mix both routes. For financial year 2026 this relief can be borrowed while staying on ESRS 2023.

The four steps

How to score

The workbook uses the scales below. They are anchors to argue from. If your enterprise risk matrix already has magnitude and likelihood bands, reuse those bands for the financial lens so the two systems agree.

Impact lens

How the impact score is built

  • Severity is the average of scale, scope and irremediability. For positive impacts, scale and scope only.
  • Actual impact: score equals severity.
  • Potential impact: score is the square root of severity times likelihood, which keeps it on the 1 to 5 scale.
  • Potential negative human-rights impact: score equals severity. Likelihood is recorded but is not allowed to pull the score down.
Financial lens

How the financial score is built

  • Score is the square root of magnitude times likelihood.
  • Magnitude covers effects on cash flows, performance, position, cost of capital and access to finance, over the horizons you tick.
  • Think about dependencies on natural, human and social resources. They are where most risks come from.
  • Score it separately. A severe impact can carry no financial consequence, and the reverse.

Judgement rules that are easy to get wrong

Set the threshold before you score

Have the scales and thresholds approved and dated first. A threshold chosen after the results are in is the first thing an assurer looks for.

Audit materiality is not a DMA threshold

A percentage of profit before tax is tuned to misstatement risk. It says nothing about whether an impact is severe.

Severity outranks likelihood for human rights

An unlikely but grave harm to people stays on the list. Both ESRS and GRI take this from the UN Guiding Principles.

Be careful what mitigation you net off

ESRS 2026 spells it out: for actual negative impacts, count only mitigation already applied in earlier periods, not this year's remediation. For potential ones, count only measures that demonstrably reduce severity or likelihood.ESRS 2023 is silent on gross versus net, which led to inconsistent practice. The 2026 text clarifies it; applying the same discipline now is sensible. A well-managed impact can still be material information.

Time horizons

Short is the reporting period, medium runs to five years, long is beyond five. You may use other horizons if you explain why. Match them to financial planning so the financial lens is consistent with the accounts.

Overrides are allowed, silently is not

Stakeholder concern or expert judgement can carry a matter over the line, or keep a borderline one out. Write the reason down. The workbook has a field for it.

How often

ESRS 2026 drops the expectation of a full annual re-run. Confirm at each reporting date that the conclusions still hold, and redo the affected parts when something changes: an acquisition or disposal, a new market or product line, a new regulation, a serious incident, or a shift in what stakeholders raise.Under ESRS 2023, practice has been to review the assessment every year and re-run it when the business, its value chain or its context changes. Either way, keep a change log against the prior year. Additions and drops nobody explained are visible to any reader holding last year's statement.

From the material list to the sustainability statement

Turn it into the report

The material list is a switch. It turns each topical standard on or off, sets how deep each disclosure goes, and is itself reportable. This section is the wiring.

The gate

Material: report it

The disclosure requirements of that standard apply, filtered by whether each piece of information is itself material. Policies, actions, targets, metrics, and value chain data where the matter sits.

Not material: say so

List the topics you assessed and found not material. For climate, ESRS 2023 asks for a detailed explanation of the conclusion.You shall not report information that isn't material. If you add some anyway, label it clearly as outside the materiality assessment. Check the final 2026 text for what explanation a not-material conclusion on climate still needs.

Not covered by any standard: build it

A material matter no ESRS covers gets entity-specific disclosure, in the same shape: policy, action, target, metric. ISSB industry guidance is an accepted source for these.

Where the assessment itself is disclosed

All of it sits in ESRS 2, which applies whatever the materiality outcome. The right-hand column is what your files from the Start here list feed into each requirement.

Changed in ESRS 2026. The list of material impacts, risks and opportunities moved from SBM-3 to IRO-2. SBM-3 now holds only their interaction with strategy and the financial effects. Governance went from five requirements to four. The topic-specific additions to IRO-1 and SBM-3 that used to sit inside each topical standard have been pulled into ESRS 2 or deleted, and climate scenario analysis is no longer mandatory.

Under ESRS 2023 each topical standard adds its own layer to IRO-1 or SBM-3: climate scenario analysis for E1, site and basin screening for E2 to E5, affected groups for S1 to S4, conduct exposure for G1. These sit in the general disclosures, which is why the method has to be documented topic by topic from the start.

The lens decides the emphasis

Impact material

Opens the management story and the impact metrics. Show what you do about the impact, not only what it costs.

  • [[MDR-P, MDR-A, MDR-T|GDR-P, GDR-A, GDR-T]] for the matter, at matter level or grouped, whichever matches how you manage it
  • Topic metrics: GHG [[E1-6|E1-8]], pollutants [[E2-4]], water [[E3-4]], biodiversity [[E4-5]], material flows [[E5-4]] and [[E5-5]]
  • Social: engagement and channels [[S1-2, S1-3|S1-2]], actions [[S1-4|S1-3]], workforce metrics from [[S1-6|S1-5]] on
  • Severity sets how much you say about actions and targets
Financially material

Opens the financial-effects disclosures and the connection to the accounts.

  • Current and anticipated financial effects: [[SBM-3]] plus [[E1-9]], [[E2-6]], [[E3-5]], [[E4-6]], [[E5-6]][[SBM-3]] for every topic, plus [[E1-11]] for climate. The separate requirements in E2 to E5 are gone.
  • Internal carbon price [[E1-8|E1-10]]; resources behind actions in [[E1-3|E1-5]]
  • Consistency with provisions, impairment, useful lives and going concern
  • Quantitative effects may be left out when they can't be separated, are too uncertain to be useful, or exceed your capabilities. Explain which.

Material on both lenses is the usual case for climate, own workforce and business conduct. Say which parts of the disclosure answer which lens, and don't let the financial framing crowd out the impact story.

The same evidence serves other regimes

EU Taxonomy

Article 8 turnover, capex and opex reporting shares the activity mapping from step 1 and still applies to companies inside the narrowed CSRD scope.

SFDR indicators

Investors read principal adverse impact figures out of E1 to E5, S1 and G1. If a topic is not material for you, they get no figure. Expect to be asked.

Due diligence law

The severity-based impact assessment and the S2 and S3 remediation content are what the CSDDD asks for. Its scope is now 5,000 employees and €1.5bn turnover, applying from July 2029.

Three frameworks, three meanings of "material"

ESRS, GRI and ISSB compared

ESRS asks two questions. GRI asks only about your impact on the world. IFRS asks only what would change an investor's decision. The assessment work is close enough to run once. The conclusions are not interchangeable.

ESRS, under the CSRD

Double materiality

Test. Material if it passes either lens: a significant actual or potential impact on people or the environment, or a risk or opportunity with a significant financial effect.

For whom. Affected stakeholders and providers of capital, on equal footing.

Force. Law for in-scope companies. Documented, justified, under limited assurance.

GRI 3 (2021)

Impact materiality

Test. Material if it reflects the organisation's most significant impacts on the economy, environment and people, including human rights. Financial consequence plays no part.

For whom. Anyone affected, and the public. Accountability, not valuation.

Force. Voluntary unless a regulator or stakeholder demands it. No assurance requirement.

IFRS: IASB and ISSB

Financial materiality

Test. Material if omitting, misstating or obscuring it could reasonably be expected to influence primary users' decisions. IFRS S1 applies the same test to sustainability information.

For whom. Investors, lenders and other creditors.

Force. Accounting standards mandatory where adopted; ISSB standards by jurisdiction.

"IFRS materiality" means two things. The accounting concept that governs what goes in the financial statements, and the ISSB's sustainability version in IFRS S1 and S2. Both are single-lens and investor-facing. ESRS overlaps with the second and has to reconcile with the first.

Where it breaks: the IFRS process is applied to information once transactions exist. ESRS and GRI are applied to topics before any data is collected. That is why a financial-reporting threshold can't be reused as an assessment threshold.

What lines up and what doesn't

Lines up

  • Severity criteria are identical in ESRS and GRI: scale, scope, irremediability, plus likelihood.
  • Value chain is in scope in both, upstream and downstream.
  • Severity outweighs likelihood for human-rights impacts in both.
  • The context step is the same work.
  • The ESRS financial lens and IFRS S1 both test effects on cash flows, access to finance and cost of capital over short, medium and long term.
  • Neither IFRS nor ESRS sets a universal number. Both want entity-specific judgement.
  • ESRS 2026 moves closer to ISSB: fair presentation, "reasonable and supportable information without undue cost or effort", the reliefs on quantifying financial effects, and a choice of GHG consolidation approach.

Doesn't

  • Lenses: GRI one, IFRS one, ESRS two. Passing either one is enough under ESRS, so its list is the longest.
  • Topic universe: GRI lets you define it. ESRS expects all ten topical standards to be considered.
  • Engagement: central and required in GRI. Expected in ESRS, weighed alongside expert judgement.
  • Exclusions: GRI lets you drop a topic silently. ESRS 2023 wants it stated, and explained for climate.ESRS wants the conclusion to be defensible and the index of reported requirements to show it.
  • Floor: GRI reports only material topics. ESRS 2 applies regardless.
  • Assurance: none required for GRI. Limited assurance for ESRS, covering the process.
  • Scenario analysis: required by IFRS S2, not by ESRS 2026.

Three traps when you reuse one assessment

Threshold transfer

Audit materiality, a small share of profit before tax, tells you nothing about whether a climate impact is material. It was set for misstatement risk.

Silent drop

A topic left out of a GRI report without comment becomes a gap under ESRS, where the conclusion has to be documented and defensible.

Single-pass scoring

Scoring impact and inferring the financial score from it. The lenses often disagree. The workbook warns you when every matter carries the same score on both.

Side by side

One assessment for all three

Build to the strictest requirement and derive the others. In practice: design for ESRS and treat GRI and ISSB as views of the same register. The workbook's GRI view and ISSB view do exactly this.

Design for

The ESRS perimeter

Consider all ten topical standards, score both lenses separately, record thresholds, evidence and stakeholder input.

Derive

The GRI view

Filter to impact-material matters and map to GRI topic standards. Method under 3-1, list under 3-2, management under 3-3.

Derive

The ISSB view

Filter to financially material risks and opportunities. The horizons and magnitude scoring already match IFRS S1.

Reconcile

The accounts

Check financially material matters against provisions, impairment, useful lives and going concern. An assurer finds a mismatch here first.

A one-way street

An ESRS-grade assessment satisfies GRI 3 and gives you most of an ISSB assessment. A GRI assessment does not satisfy ESRS: no financial lens, no duty to consider the full topic list, no need to justify what was left out. Upgrading is a new scoring pass and a new documentation standard, not a relabelling.
